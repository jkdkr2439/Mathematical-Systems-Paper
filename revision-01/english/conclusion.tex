\section{Limits and open problems}
The finite coding results use fixed-length deterministic messages and a worst-case correctness requirement. With a probability distribution, variable lengths, block codes, or an error probability, the optimization problem changes. For stochastic transitions one must also decide whether to preserve distributions or individual paths.

State-dependent events can make enabledness itself informative. A state augmentation or general finite partition can handle some such cases, but a single row space may cease to characterize the task. Vector resources give a partially ordered family of admissible languages. Efficiently representing its nondominated budgets remains an algorithmic question.

The arithmetic result provides matching bounds under dual bad approximability and a fixed-alphabet obstruction using Liouville approximation. It leaves intermediate arithmetic classes unresolved. Finite measurements alone cannot establish that a physical quantity equals a specific irrational number or give an all-scale Diophantine constant. The finite supplied-value certificate is available when only finite-scale evidence is justified.

The remeasurement analysis gives an exact optimum with two fresh labels and unrestricted receiver feedback, and an approximation guarantee for a finite question alphabet. General finite questions, other fresh alphabets, noisy links, memory limits, synchronization, and measured energy require additional analysis. The stated evidence establishes no field performance or historical priority. The observability, factorization, sensitivity, and greedy arguments have explicit antecedents. The specialized asymptotic counts and timing formulas are stated in a form suitable for direct comparison with prior results.

\section{Conclusion}
The application tests show why the endpoint matters. On the held-out CDN trace, a nominal mistake often moved a window by just one request. The consequences for incident triage require operator evidence. In the packet experiment the decoder still reconstructed the sample within the stated error after its deadline had expired. An accurate answer about a past sample therefore requires a separate justification before use for a present action.
The quantity of information required for a decision depends on which states are feasible, which interactions can occur, when the information will be used, and which answers the task accepts. For exact observations these choices specify a quotient. For finite affine systems the quotient admits an explicit rank calculation and compressed update. For decisions admitting several answers, the relevant condition is the existence of a common action. For measured inputs, a bound on reconstruction and drift becomes a positional decision certificate only after its uncertainty is evaluated on the competitors' score differences.

A changing physical system continues to evolve during observation, transmission, and computation. A proof certifies the consequences of a specified observation model over its stated domain. Applying it later requires evidence that the bounds still hold at the time of action. A system can use a new measurement, revise the admissible model, or decline an expired certificate. These choices give the distinction between a mathematical guarantee and a claim about the world an operational meaning.
